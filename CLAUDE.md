# Raindrop of the Month — project rules

This folder holds Rainmaker Securities' monthly one-page company showcase. Read README.md for
the folder layout and editing map. These rules capture what Ben decided over the first issue.

## What the deliverable is
- One self-contained `raindrop.html` per month in a `YYYY-MM-company/` folder, plus `raindrop.pdf`
  and `preview.png` generated by `./render.sh <folder>`.
- Since Nov 2026 the words live in the month's `content.md` and `render.sh` regenerates
  `raindrop.html` from it via `build.py` + the shared `template.html` (Ben wanted to edit text
  himself without asking). Never edit `raindrop.html` by hand; it is overwritten on every render.
  Text changes go in `content.md`; design and CSS changes go in `template.html`; new section
  types or formats go in `build.py` (and get documented in README "Editing an issue").
  The hand-written page before the switch is in `2026-10-anduril/archive/before-content-file-2026-11-06.html`.
- Must print to exactly one US Letter page. In print the page box no longer clips overflow, so
  `render.sh` reports a second page honestly; always look at the PDF bottom (`sips -s format png
  --resampleWidth 1700 <folder>/raindrop.pdf --out /tmp/p.png`) after adding text. Screen edition uses readable, unscaled type, a
  1280px maximum width. Only the footer trade bar stays pinned on screen; the header scrolls
  away with the content (Ben's call, Oct 2026).
- Start a new month by copying the latest month's folder, not from scratch.

## Brand
- Rainmaker Securities is the publisher. "Rainmaker-X" is the exchange and appears only in the
  footer call to action. Never brand the header as Rainmaker X.
- Header: dark navy bar `#061530`, official Rainmaker Securities R mark (from `assets/rainmaker_official_gold.svg`),
  "RAINMAKER SECURITIES" in white Jost 600, with RAINMAKER letter-spacing .15em, "Raindrop of the Month" centered, month in white right.
- Header title uses Jost 600, matching the top-right date. The introduction starts with the full "Rainmaker Securities" name in larger navy Jost type. The hero places the large drop beside the centered official Anduril wordmark and subtitle, with a divider underneath; the introduction and tags sit in their own row below. The header title reads "Raindrop of the Month" without decorative raindrops. Do not repeat the monthly heading in the hero.
- Colors come from the exchange's CSS: page `#eef5fb`, ink `#1b1f24`, navy `#10305f`,
  deepest navy `#061530` for the company name and big numbers, gold `#b48a2f` / `#d4af37`.
  No beige or cream anywhere.
- Font: Jost (Google Fonts). The public site uses Futura PT; Jost is the free match.
- Shared logos live in `brand/`. Company-specific assets live in the month's `assets/`.

## The raindrop
- Rain-colored: light-to-deep water-blue gradient, white gloss highlight, thin gold rim, soft blue shadow.
- The company's logo sits inside it, recolored to navy, as a transparent PNG; the abbreviated issue month sits beneath it in dark navy.
- No ripple rings or circles under it.

## Content and tone
- Rainmaker presents and brokers securities. It does not recommend or speculate.
  No bull case, bear case, "why we're watching", "the honest part", or price opinions.
  The disclaimer must say "not a recommendation".
- Professional and concise with at most one dry line of humor. Section titles in plain
  analyst language: "What they build" (not "it"), "2026 so far", "Lattice: AI central command".
- "What they build" is six sections (Autonomous aircraft, Missiles & interceptors, Sensors &
  scanners, Air & space defense, Underwater vehicles, Soldier & command systems), each a bulleted
  list with one bullet per product: bold product name, colon, one short plain phrase. Every
  product goes in a category (Ben's call); no separate "other" list. Two to five bullets per
  section. Keep bullets short so the print column fits.
- Under the photo sits "Customers": one-line bullets of who buys what (services, agencies,
  countries). It fills the column under the photo; on screen the photo stretches so the left
  and right columns end together, in print the photo has a fixed height.
  Avoid anything that reads as AI-written: no "game-changer", no "in today's world", no em dashes.
- Intro paragraph: opens "Rainmaker Securities presents <Company> as the <Month Year> Raindrop of
  the Month.", then founding year, what the company builds and why it matters, and it ends with
  a line that it is one of the most requested names on our desk. No pop-culture references
  (Ben's boss rejected a Terminator line, Oct 2026). No bold product terms, no colored or oversized first letter. Ben dictates this
  paragraph's wording; fix only grammar and capitalization. Serial comma.
- Tags (chips) are all the same plain white style. No colored variants.
- Photos (Ben's calls, Oct 2026): hardware in action and no people in frame. Missiles firing,
  interceptors launching, drones in flight, rocket launches, submarines going in the water, the
  software on a big screen. Show variety, not one product type. Use public-domain U.S. government
  photos (DVIDS is the main source; CBP and Space Force too) or CC-BY, with the credit in the
  caption. No company press or marketing images unless Ben says so for that month.
- DVIDS download: `https://d1ldvf68ux039x.cloudfront.net/thumbs/photos/<YYMM posted>/<id>/2000w_q95.jpg`
  (the /download/ link needs a login; 2000w is the largest open size). media.defense.gov blocks curl.
  Portrait photos do not work in the wide carousel box; crop out bystanders rather than skip a shot.
- Lattice is software, so the only usable public-domain picture is the ABMS wall display (Air Force,
  White Sands 2020), cropped to the screen; it is kept as the second slide. Anduril's own satellite
  photos are company images, so the satellite story is told with the Space Force launch photo.
- Verify every number with a web search for that month. Date the figures. Label reported
  but unconfirmed rounds as unconfirmed. Use the company's legal name (e.g. Anduril Industries).

## Workflow
- Delivery is the web link. Ben sends the interactive page to clients as a newsletter, so it must
  work for everyone: https://benmartinrainmaker.github.io/rainmaker-raindrop/<folder>/ . Since
  Nov 11, 2026 this whole folder is the git repo BenMartinRainmaker/rainmaker-raindrop (Rainmaker
  GitHub account, never the personal one; `gh auth switch -u BenMartinRainmaker` before pushing).
  Every push to main runs `.github/workflows/publish.yml`, which renders each month, runs `stage.sh`
  (noindex tag, version-stamped image URLs, root redirect), and deploys Pages. `./publish.sh "msg"`
  pulls, commits everything, and pushes. Generated files are gitignored; so are `archive/` folders.
  The URL never changes. If someone reports broken photos right after a publish, they opened it in
  the minute before the images reached the CDN: reopen the link.
- Web edit mode (Ben's ask, Nov 2026, so a group can edit without tools): the live page plus
  `?edit=1` makes every text block click-to-type and "Save & publish" commits `content.md` through
  the GitHub API with a shared fine-grained token (Contents read/write on this repo only) that the
  editor pastes once. `build.py` tags each element with `data-ed="kind:line..."` pointing at its
  content.md line; the JS in `template.html` patches only changed lines, refuses to save if the
  repo's content.md no longer matches the one the page was built from, and offers a download
  fallback. Photos, chart data, and logos are not web-editable. Keep the `.quote .who` class and the
  data-ed tagging intact when touching build.py.
- `raindrop.html` loads images from `assets/` by relative path, so it breaks when sent on its own.
  `render.sh` also writes `share/Rainmaker-Raindrop-<folder>.html` with every image embedded
  (via `inline.py`), the PDF next to it, and `share/Rainmaker-Raindrop-<folder>-web.zip` (page, images,
  PDF only; nothing else from the project) for whoever puts it on the company website. Attachments are the fallback only: email preview
  panes (Outlook, Gmail) strip embedded images and scripts, and the PDF is static (no carousel).
- The Rainmaker X exchange prototype is a separate project in `../X` with its own publish script
  and docs; do not document it here.
  To take it all off the web: `gh auth switch -u BenMartinRainmaker`, then
  `gh auth refresh -h github.com -s delete_repo` (one-time, interactive), then
  `gh repo delete BenMartinRainmaker/rainmaker-raindrop --yes`. Faster: make the repo private on github.com, which turns Pages off.
- Edit `content.md` (or `template.html` for design), run `./render.sh <folder>`, look at `preview.png`, then
  `open -a "Google Chrome" <folder>/raindrop.html` so Ben can see it. Then `./publish.sh` to put it live.
- Phones (screen width 900px or less) use normal document scrolling with a sticky footer instead
  of the fixed-height inner scroller, because iOS Safari clips that layout behind its toolbar.
  Test on a phone with the iOS Simulator: serve the folder with `python3 -m http.server 8765`,
  `xcrun simctl boot <iPhone udid>`, `xcrun simctl openurl booted http://localhost:8765/share/<file>`,
  wait ~25s for the share file to load, then `xcrun simctl io booted screenshot out.png`.
- The Chrome extension cannot open file:// URLs. To test the carousel or scaling in the
  extension, serve the folder with `python3 -m http.server <port>` and stop it afterward.
- Keep earlier takes in the month's `archive/` folder rather than deleting them.
